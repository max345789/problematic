Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) Standards

Last Updated: November 20, 2026 · App: Problematic (com.problematic.app) · Developer: Sarath C

Strict Zero Tolerance Policy

Problematic strictly prohibits any form of Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) and Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE). Any attempt to produce, distribute, solicit, or facilitate child exploitation results in permanent termination, content purging, and immediate referral to law enforcement and child protection bodies.

1. Policy Commitment

Problematic is a community dedicated to real-life challenges. We place child safety at the absolute center of our platform architecture. Under no circumstances is content or communication tolerated that endangers minors or involves sexual abuse, grooming, exploitation, or trafficking of children.

2. Immediate Enforcement & Account Ban Actions

Any individual found attempting to violate child safety standards will face:

  • Instant, permanent account termination: The offending user is barred immediately from the platform with no opportunity for appeal.
  • Complete data purge: Associated media, posts, replies, and direct messages are removed immediately from public and private view.
  • Evidence preservation: Relevant metadata and logs are preserved strictly for handing over to authorized legal entities.

3. In-App Reporting Mechanisms

Problematic provides integrated, easily accessible reporting tools on every problem card, reply thread, direct message session, and user profile:

  • Users can click "Report" → select "Child Safety / CSAE".
  • Reports are routed immediately to an expedited high-priority moderation queue.
  • Moderators and system safeguards take immediate action to isolate the content and suspend the author.

4. Cooperation with NCMEC & Global Law Enforcement

In accordance with United States federal law (18 U.S.C. § 2258A) and international child safety standards, Problematic reports any confirmed or suspected incident of CSAM/CSAE directly to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) via their CyberTipline, as well as competent local and international law enforcement agencies.
